Excel workbook for quotes

A quote should start from the client's request, not from last month's file. This workbook builds each quote from codes and quantities: price lists, machining cycles and quantity discounts live once, in linked sheets.

Quotes from shared price lists and formulas, for a fictitious mechanical SME with about 50 employees.

Step 1 of 17

  1. The sheet you fill in

    What does whoever writes the quote type?

    Only item code and quantity for each row, plus the client details. Everything else, in the coloured cells, is calculated from the linked sheets.

  2. Number and revision

    Which version am I looking at?

    Revision and date are read from the revision log, and the validity date adds the days set among the parameters.

  3. Description and material

    Do I have to copy the item card?

    No: from the code, a lookup formula fetches description and material from the Items sheet. A wrong code shows up at once.

  4. Cost per piece

    Where does the cost come from?

    Material: weight times price per kg, plus scrap. Machining: cycle minutes times the hourly rate of each work centre, plus setup spread over the pieces.

  5. Quantity discount

    Which discount applies to this quantity?

    The tier table decides: at 600 pins the 9% tier applies, without anyone remembering it.

  6. Total

    How much is the quote worth?

    Price with markup and discount, subtotal, transport and total. The total also lands in the revision log.

  7. Items and cycles

    How is each part made?

    For each code: material, weight and minutes on lathe, milling, grinding, welding and heat treatment.

  8. The machining cycle

    How much machine time does it take?

    Engineering writes the minutes per piece once for each item. The quote reads them from here.

  9. Calculated costs

    How much does machining cost?

    Minutes times the rate of each centre, with setup kept apart: change one rate and every item that uses it follows.

  10. Price lists and parameters

    Where do you change a price?

    Here and only here: materials, work centres, quantity tiers and parameters such as markup, transport and validity.

  11. Materials

    Has the supplier raised the price of steel?

    Update the price per kg with its date: S355 moved to €1.55/kg on 27 October, and new quotes use it straight away.

  12. Work centres

    What does a machine hour cost?

    One rate per centre, shared by every item and every quote.

  13. Quantity tiers

    From how many pieces does the discount change?

    Four thresholds, read by an approximate-match lookup: no discounts decided case by case.

  14. Parameters

    Which markup do we apply?

    Markup, setup rate, transport and validity sit in one small table, each with a name the formulas call.

  15. Revision log

    What changed since the first draft?

    Each revision is a row: date, change, who made it and total. No copies of the file to compare.

  16. The story of a quote

    Why did the price go up?

    Quote P-2026-0139 moved from S235 to S355 at the client's request: the reason stays written next to the total.

  17. The current revision

    Is the log up to date?

    The last row reads its total straight from the quote; earlier rows keep the value they had at the time.

Who it is for, and what they do with it

Three offices work on the same workbook, each on its own part. Nobody retypes what someone else has already written.

Answer the request with the right figures

Sales office

Types codes and quantities, reads cost, discount and total. When the client changes a quantity, the new revision is one row in the log.

Describe each part once

Engineering

Material, weight and cycle minutes for each item. Every quote that uses that code reads the same cycle.

Keep prices and rules in one place

Purchasing and management

Material prices, hourly rates, markup and quantity tiers. A change made here applies to every new quote.

Look up

From the code, description, material, weight and cycle, without copying.

Calculate

Material, machining, setup and markup with the same formulas for every row.

Apply tiers

The discount follows the quantity, read from a table instead of from memory.

Track

Every revision with date, reason and total, in the same file.

How it works

No macros and no add-ins: structured tables, lookup formulas and named cells. It opens in any recent version of Excel, and whoever uses it can read every formula.

Possible benefits

When prices and rules live in one place, a quote stops being a copy to check. Flip the switch and compare the two situations.

  • Before Every quote starts from a copy of the previous one, with price lists and formulas to recheck.

    After Every quote starts from the same price lists and the same formulas, without copying data.

  • Before Every price list change has to be carried over by hand into the sheets in use.

    After Every price list change is made in one place and applies to new quotes.

  • Before Every quote revision leaves several file versions to tell apart.

    After Every quote revision stays tracked in the same sheet.

Use case with fictitious data. The benefits described are possible, not results achieved with a client.
